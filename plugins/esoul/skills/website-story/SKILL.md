# The story comes first

A site sells one feeling. Find it, then write every line toward it. This is the method
externalsoul.com was written with; the website entry skill is `website`, the next stage is
`website-look`.

## 1. The interview (ask, don't assume)

Ask the owner, one message, and wait:

1. Who is this for? Name 1–4 kinds of visitor, concretely ("a café owner in Brno", not "SMBs").
2. For each: what do they want when they arrive, and what should they DO (book, order, write,
   sign up, read)?
3. What is true about you that nobody else can say? (the place, the craft, the numbers)
4. Words you love, words you hate. The language(s) of the audience.
5. What do you already have: photos, logo, films, a site to keep or to beat?

## 2. The one feeling

Write it as one sentence a visitor might think: *"this is the computer I wished I had"*,
*"this is where I want my wedding flowers from"*. Every chapter serves it. If you cannot
write it, you do not have the story yet — ask again.

## 3. Chapters

One chapter per audience (or per reason to stay), each with:

- a **promise** in the visitor's words — ≤ 8 words, concrete, a verb: *"Set a goal. Give it a
  budget. Come back to answers."*
- **proof**: what they will see — a film, a photo, three numbers, a quote;
- **one action** — the button label a visitor would say out loud ("Book a table", not "Submit").

Order: the widest audience first, the most specific last, and a closing chapter that repeats
the main action. Five to seven chapters is a whole page; more is a second page.

## 4. The hero

- **Eyebrow** ≤ 24 characters — what it is ("A private computer in the cloud").
- **Title** ≤ 30 characters per line, two lines at most — the feeling, not the feature.
- **Line under it** ≤ 120 characters — what it does and for whom, plainly.
- **Two actions**: the main one, and a quieter one ("See how it works").

## 5. The voice

- Concrete nouns and verbs. "Your agents answer the mail while you sleep", not "leverage
  automation".
- Short sentences. One idea each. Read it aloud: if you run out of breath, split it.
- No filler: *seamless, powerful, cutting-edge, unlock, elevate, revolutionise*.
- No false urgency, no invented numbers, no testimonials the owner did not give you.
- Headings survive a phone: ≤ 44 characters for a section heading.

## 6. Hand-off

Write the whole page's copy into one document (a block-notes page in the workspace, or the
chat) as: chapter → promise → proof → action → body text. The owner approves it before
`website-look`. Changing words after the page is built is cheap; changing the story is not.
